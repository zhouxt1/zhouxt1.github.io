About me

I am a PhD Candidate in Computer Science and Engineering at Pennsylvania State University. Prior to joining Penn State, I obtained my B.E. in Computer Science and Technology at ShanghaiTech University.

I am interested in Computer Security and Programming Languages. Specifically, I work on verified software systems (parsers), bug finding methods (fuzzing, differential testing) and Undefined Behaviors in ‘safe’ programming languages like Rust. I am advised by Dr. Syed Hussain and Dr. Gary G. Tan.

Publications

ACM CCS 2026 arXiv:2608.09094

VUPER: Verified ASN.1 UPER Parser

Xiaotian Zhou, Kai Tu, Ali Ranjbar, Yilu Dong, Gang Tan, Syed Rafiul Hussain

A framework for generating verified ASN.1 UPER parsers, plus a differential testing harness that uses the verified parser as its oracle. It uncovered 20 types of inconsistency across 11 open-source and commercial parsers on 5G and V2X protocols, with working attacks.

Research

Verified parsers for binary protocols

Rocq Prover · OCaml · 2023–present

Bit-precise parser correctness, round-trip consistency between parsers and serializers, and a compiler that turns an ASN.1 specification directly into a verified parser. See VUPER.

Undefined behavior in “safe” languages

Rust · Verus

Safety guarantees stop at the unsafe boundary, and in practice that boundary leaks. I study how undefined behavior reaches supposedly safe Rust, and what it takes to state and discharge the obligations that keep it out.

Bug finding for protocol implementations

Fuzzing · Differential testing

Verification tells you an implementation matches a specification. Differential testing tells you which of several implementations is lying. A verified reference implementation makes a very good oracle for the second.

Adversarial attacks on audio spoofing countermeasures

ShanghaiTech · Undergraduate thesis · 2022–2023

Adversarial attacks against countermeasure models protecting automatic speaker verification, advised by Prof. Fu Song.
